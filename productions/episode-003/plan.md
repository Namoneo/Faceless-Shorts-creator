## Why these rounds

Uzbek YouTube research for this series found that locally debatable everyday choices outperform generic quizzes. Episode 1 led with the proven Gentra/Cobalt car pair. This episode leads with another everyday car rivalry, Nexia and Malibu, then interleaves food, apps, fruit, a teahouse, a wedding greeting, and two national holidays, so the car round stands alone.

Rounds, in order:

1. Nexia YOKI Malibu
2. Lagʻmon YOKI shashlik
3. Telegram YOKI Instagram
4. Qovun YOKI tarvuz
5. Choyxona YOKI restoran
6. Anʼanaviy kelin salom YOKI qisqa zamonaviy salom
7. Navroʻz YOKI Yangi yil

## Facts marked in the script

Spoken claims, and where they were checked. The Tekshiruv section of `script.md` holds the same notes and the source URLs. Nothing in that section is read aloud.

1. Nexia and Malibu. Serial production of Nexia started in 1996 at the Asaka plant (then Uz-Daewoo Auto). Large-block assembly of the Chevrolet Malibu started in Uzbekistan in 2012. Official archive: March 2012. A plant history on UzDaily says February 2012; the Tashkent presentation was 20 March 2012. Early assembly was reported at the Tashkent aviation enterprise, so the script does not say both cars were made in Asaka. It says both were produced in Uzbekistan, and speaks only the years. No engine, price, or quality comparison.
2. Lagʻmon and shashlik. Lagʻmon dough is made by hand; it is stretched or cut, and served with meat and vegetables. Shashlik is cooked over coals on a mangal. The spoken line avoids the Uzbek word «six» (skewer), which can be misread in Latin type. Origin legends are not spoken.
3. Telegram and Instagram. Instagram launched on 6 October 2010. Telegram for iOS launched on 14 August 2013. The script speaks only the years, and says «ishga tushgan» for Instagram and «chiqqan» for Telegram. No user-count, quality, or market-share comparison.
4. Qovun and tarvuz. Official Uzbekistan Travel: Ibn Battuta travelled through Central Asia in 1333–1335 and, in his chapter on Urgench, described the Khorezm melon with admiration and noted that it was dried into a sweet. Dried melon is still eaten in Uzbekistan. The script says «the fourteenth century», not the exact years. No claim that the melon was the sweetest in the world. No annual watermelon tonnage is spoken.
5. Choyxona and restoran. Official Uzbekistan Travel: the choyxona has been a gathering place for centuries. In the past, in large cities, teahouses stood beside caravanserais. People drank tea, heard news, and made agreements there. The three-pour tea ritual is not spoken in this episode, because it was already the pilot fact for choy/qatiq.
6. Kelin salom. The national ICH entry «Nikoh» lists kelin salom among wedding ceremonies and says it was held the day after the wedding, with bet ochar. The «Yuz ochdi» entry says that after the meal the bride greets the groom's close relatives one by one and receives their gifts. No guest-count is spoken. No religious explanation is spoken.
7. Navroʻz and Yangi yil. gov.uz lists 1 January as New Year and 21 March as the Navruz Holiday; both are fixed official public holidays. UNESCO inscribed Nowruz on the Representative List in 2009 (4.COM 13.03), with Uzbekistan among the submitting states. The script speaks the two dates, the year 2009, and that both are official holidays. The pilot's sumalak method is not repeated.
