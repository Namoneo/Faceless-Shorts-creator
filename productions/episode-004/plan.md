## Why these rounds

Uzbek YouTube research for this series found that locally debatable everyday choices outperform generic quizzes. Episodes 1 and 2 led with passenger-car pairs. This episode keeps a vehicle hook, but switches to the everyday work-and-family rivalry of Damas and Labo, then interleaves food, a payment app, bread, a journey, a dance, and two historic cities, so the vehicle round stands alone.

Rounds, in order:

3. Click YOKI Payme
4. Samarqand noni YOKI Toshkent noni
5. Poyezd YOKI samolyot
6. Lazgi YOKI zamonaviy raqs
7. Xiva YOKI Shahrisabz

None of the pilot rounds is reused (palov/somsa, choy/qatiq, Samarqand/Buxoro, doira/rubob, kurash/futbol, sumalak/holva, bozor/hovli). ## Facts marked in the script

Spoken claims, and where they were checked. The Tekshiruv section of `script.md` holds the same notes and the source URLs. Nothing in that section is read aloud.

1. Damas and Labo. Damas production in Uzbekistan started in 1996 (March at Asaka; the plant's official serial start with Nexia, Damas and Tico is given as 19 July 1996). Serial production of Labo started on 27 July 2015 at the Khorezm Auto plant. The script says both were produced in Uzbekistan, and speaks only the years. No engine, payload, price, or quality comparison. The later move of Damas production to Khorezm is not spoken.
2. Norin and dimlama. Explore Uzbekistan: norin is a Tashkent dish of hair-thin cold noodles and finely sliced boiled meat, served cool with a bowl of hot broth. The script does not name the meat. Dimlama is layered meat and vegetables cooked in a kazan under a lid, in their own juices, without added water; the name comes from dimlamoq / dimlash, to cook slowly in steam. Cooking times and a full ingredient list are not spoken.
3. Click and Payme. Click's official site says the company was established in November 2011. Gazeta.uz (11 November 2015) also dates the system's launch to November 2011. The Payme mobile app was released on Google Play on 25 March 2016 (Gazeta.uz, 30 March 2022). The script says Click was established in 2011 and the Payme app came out in 2016. No user-count, market-share, or quality comparison.
4. Samarqand non and Toshkent non. Official Uzbekistan Travel: in the Samarkand, Bukhara and Kashkadarya regions the tandir is set vertically; in Tashkent and the Fergana regions it is set horizontally against a wall. The script speaks only that placement. No claim about taste, or that one loaf is the usual travel gift.
5. Train and plane. Official Uzbekistan Railways passenger history: the Afrosiyob high-speed train was launched in 2011 on the Tashkent–Samarkand–Tashkent route. UzDaily gives the start of regular service as 8 October 2011. The script speaks the year and the Tashkent–Samarkand route, not the exact day or a «first in Central Asia» claim. Uzbekistan Airways' official history dates the airline to 1992; the company marked its 33rd anniversary on 28 January 2025. The script speaks only the year.
6. Lazgi and modern dance. UNESCO inscribed «Khorazm dance, Lazgi» on the Representative List in 2019 (14.COM). The entry says the dance was initially associated with the Khorezm region and has since become widespread in Uzbekistan. The script speaks that spread and the year 2019. No century-count, and no ranking of dance styles.
7. Xiva and Shahrisabz. UNESCO inscribed Itchan Kala (Ichon-qalʼa) in Khiva in 1990 (14 COM VII.A) and the Historic Centre of Shakhrisyabz in 2000 (24 COM X.C.1). The script speaks only those years. Wall height, a «first site» claim, and the later danger-list status of Shahrisabz are not spoken.
